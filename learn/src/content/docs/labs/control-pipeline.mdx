## What you're about to control

This lab runs the real RoveLink browser-side control pipeline — **InputOwnership**, **ControlEngine**, **ControlSender** — and simulates the network, relay, and firmware past that boundary. The differential mix is the real `differentialMix()` from `@rovelink/protocol`.

## Try this

1. Press **Z** to arm the robot (watch the `armed` state change)
2. Hold **W** to drive forward — observe the throttle value rise
3. Press **A** or **D** to turn — watch steering change and wheels differentiate
4. Hold the stick steady and observe the sender decision cycle between **rate** and **heartbeat**
5. Press **Space** to stop — note the immediate send on braking
6. Try a second input source (touch pad or gamepad) — watch ownership transfer
7. Try **Cut connection** while driving — see the robot stop on its own

## What to notice

- **Ownership**: only one source drives at a time — try holding a key, then touching the pad
- **ControlSender**: the send-rhythm decision (immediate / rate / heartbeat) using the real `decideSend()`
- **Firmware (sim)**: session, sequence, and disarmed-baseline checks, reproducing `applyControlFrame()` from the real firmware
- **Differential mix**: the real `differentialMix()` from `@rovelink/protocol` — identical to what the firmware runs

## What should you have noticed?

- Ownership transferred when you used a second input source
- The engine stored the latest state — no queue accumulated
- The sender decided "immediate" on arm/disarm/stop, "rate" on movement, "heartbeat" while held steady
- The frame carried seq, sentAt, ttlMs — the relay stamped controlSessionId
- The firmware validated session and seq before accepting
- The differential mix converted your throttle/steering into left/right wheel speeds
- The ACK confirmed the robot applied your command
